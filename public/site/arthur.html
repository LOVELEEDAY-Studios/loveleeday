Watch Arthur work through a question

Ask in plain words. Arthur works out the smaller questions behind yours, checks each one against your records, and answers with where every piece came from.

Someone asks

Which guests arriving this weekend have special requests?

First it checks

Who arrives between Friday and Sunday?

Then

Which bookings include a request?

Then

Which guests are filed under two different names?

Finally

Which requests has the front desk already handled?

Arthur answers

Nine arrivals have requests. Three have not reached the front desk, and two belong to returning guests filed under an old name.

A small hotel, from its bookings, messages and guest notes. Example data.

The same care on every kind of question

Each example shows what Arthur checks first and what to do with the result.

A multi-site retailer asks

What do we know about this customer, and can I trust it?

  • Which records are about the same customer?
  • What is missing, out of date, or in conflict?
  • What has this customer bought and asked about?
  • Can I see where each detail came from?

Arthur answers

Four records belong to one customer, and two details disagree. Check the linked records before choosing a primary contact.

Sources: customer records, billing, support tickets. Example data.

A shop owner asks

Where are we losing margin on what we sell?

  • What does each item cost us to deliver?
  • How do discounts, returns and fees eat into it?
  • Which prices sit below cost or below our target?
  • Which records support each flag?

Arthur answers

Several items sell below target once discounts and fees are counted. Review those first, then decide which prices to change.

Sources: item costs, what customers paid, margin targets. Example data.

A property group asks

Which of our locations are using more energy than a year ago?

  • Which locations and meters are in scope, under any name?
  • How does each month compare with last year?
  • What changed at the locations that went up?
  • Which bill or reading supports each figure?

Arthur answers

Three locations are up. One added a second tenant, and one has a meter that was read twice in March. Fix the double reading before you compare.

Sources: utility bills, meter readings. Example data.

More context makes for better questions

A useful answer starts before the sentence is written. Are these records about the same organization? What was true at the time? What changed? Arthur checks who the records concern, what was known then, and what the sources support, before it answers you. The same care works on contracts, work orders, invoices, inventory and project records, wherever your data lives.
