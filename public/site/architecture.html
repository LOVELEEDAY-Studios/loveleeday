Check an answer before you act on it.

You can see why an answer is reliable before you act on it: who it concerns, where each figure came from, when it was true, and what has changed since. Five layers make that possible. Each one is described below, with the screen of the portal where you meet it.

Explore the layers ↓

Different names for one subject

A company can be a familiar name in conversation, a customer ID in a payment record and a supplier in your ledger. Arthur matches them to one company and keeps every original name.

Where Arthur is not sure of a match, it asks a person instead of merging.

In practice

HES, Harbor Equipment Services and its vendor ID become one company record.

Every fact has a way back to its source

Every figure carries the system and record it came from, so whoever reviews an answer knows where to look.

If a source is missing, the answer says so instead of sounding sure.

What was true, and what was known

For every fact we keep two dates: when it was true and when we learned it. That lets you judge a past decision by what was known at the time.

A later correction adds information. It does not change what was known earlier.

A claim needs something behind it.

Before a figure goes into an answer, Arthur checks it against the recorded value. If nothing supports it, it stays out until something does.

A polished sentence and a supported statement are different things. Each claim is tied to a named subject and a recorded value.

When facts change, attention follows.

You tell Arthur which changes matter to a decision. When a new record changes the picture, Arthur brings the decision back to you.

You decide what deserves attention, what evidence comes with it, and who reviews the next step.

You approve what could affect people or money.

Arthur sorts every action by what it could change if it were wrong. Routine work can run on its own. Anything that moves money, reaches someone, or binds the business waits for a person, with the evidence in front of them.
