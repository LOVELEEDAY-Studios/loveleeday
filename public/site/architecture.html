Five layers make every answer traceable.

Arthur identifies what each record refers to, keeps its source and history, checks each figure, and tells the right person when something changes.

Explore the layersTalk to us
Identity

Every name points to one subject.

A company can be a familiar name in a conversation, a customer number in a payment system and a supplier record in the ledger. Arthur connects those references to one subject.

The original identifiers are kept, and uncertain matches remain available for a person to review.

In practice

HES and Harbor Equipment Services resolve to one company record.

Source

Every fact keeps a link to its source.

Each fact records the system and the record that support it, which gives anyone reviewing an answer a place to begin.

When a source is missing, the answer says so, and the gap stays visible.

In practice

Each fact is stored with its source system and the exact source record.

Memory

The record separates what was true from what was known.

Arthur stores when a fact was true and when your organization learned it, which preserves the context of a past decision.

A later correction adds to the record and leaves intact what people could have known at the time.

In practice

A payment example keeps both the earlier balance of $902.58 and the later balance of $0.00.

Verification

Every figure is checked before it reaches an answer.

Arthur checks each figure against the recorded values. A figure with no support stays out of the answer until evidence for it appears.

Each statement is tied to a named subject and a recorded value, so a well-written sentence cannot stand in for evidence.

In practice

A claim that 4,000 records were resolved is flagged as unsupported, because nothing recorded backs it.

Awareness

When a fact changes, the right person is told.

Automatic checks describe the changes that matter to a decision. A new record can bring an earlier assumption back to the person who made it.

You define what deserves attention, which evidence should come with it and who reviews the next step.

In practice

An example check: an item’s cost rises above its actual selling price.
