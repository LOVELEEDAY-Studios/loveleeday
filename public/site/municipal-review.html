Use cases / Municipal review

Know which rules apply and what is missing

Find which local rules may still apply, what changed, and what the responsible reviewer needs to check. LOVELEEDAY organizes the record, traces amendments and flags the open questions.

Shape a reviewSee how a rule is traced

Example answer for a city clerk

Which rules about permit terms changed last year? Three. One chapter still cites the old term.

Send that chapter to the legal reviewer before anyone relies on it. Sources: adopted ordinances, amendments, code register. Fictional sample.

What applies, and what changed

A review needs a defined jurisdiction, the source documents and a record of what is missing. Start with the code, then connect the amendments, repeals and the places that refer to them. The useful result is simple: which rule may have changed, with the cited issue ready for the reviewer.

Every version, and what cites it

A provision is an original, its amendments and the places that refer to it. The register keeps them together, each tied to its source document.

  1. Original textThe base recordOrdinance 2011-14, section 4.2
  2. Amendment 1Changes the permit termOrdinance 2016-07, amending 4.2
  3. Amendment 2Another chapter still cites the old termOrdinance 2019-22, chapter 9

The questions a review actually asks

From scattered files to a cited review

Section 4.2 was amended twice. Chapter 9 still quotes the first version. Here are the passages.

Establish coverage. List the document sources, versions, jurisdiction and the gaps we already know about.

Follow the changes. Connect amendments, repeals, replaced provisions and cross-references to the documents behind them.

Prepare the review. Produce a cited register of issues, with source passages and questions for the responsible reviewer.
