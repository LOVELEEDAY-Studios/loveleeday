What it checks

Six checks, each with its rows.

  • Prices below current cost. Needs item, price and cost. A dollar loss needs units sold; without them you see exposure per unit.
  • Stale prices. Needs a price date and a last sale date.
  • Products that have stopped selling. Needs quantity on hand and a last sale date.
  • Duplicate customers. Customer names that differ only in punctuation, spacing or legal suffix.
  • Inactive customers holding prices. Needs a customer status column.
  • Prices not updated after a cost increase. Needs cost history, so it runs on connected systems, not on a single-cost file.
How to read it

We refuse a figure we cannot source.

  • Loss is computed on real sales volume in your file. Exposure is a per-unit gap with no volume behind it, and is never added into a total.
  • Every count opens to its rows, numbered as in your file, and exports to CSV.
  • The executive brief is written from templates; every number in it is read from the computed result.
  • Market movement, when shown, is a public index. It is context, never a statement about your own cost.
  • Your file and result are stored privately and deleted seven days after upload.
