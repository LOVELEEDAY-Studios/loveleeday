Why LOVELEEDAY exists

Too many decisions.
Not enough time.

We built LOVELEEDAY because we realized most organizations have too many decisions to make and not enough time to make them, and more information than they have people to work through it. The answers are usually already in their systems. No one has the hours to pull them together.

Talk to us ↗

More room for the moments that matter.

What we kept seeing
  1. Decisions pile up faster than answers

    Owners and managers make dozens of calls a week, most of them with half the picture, because the full picture takes too long to build.

  2. Too much information, not enough people

    Sales, accounting, scheduling, email and documents all hold part of the answer. Very few teams have someone whose job is to put it together.

  3. The report takes longer than the decision

    People spend days gathering numbers for a choice that takes ten minutes once the facts are in front of them.

  4. Small teams, big-company complexity

    A twenty-person organization faces the same suppliers, rules and reporting as a large one, without the analysts to keep up.

So we built Arthur

The answer, already
worked out.

Arthur reads the systems you already use and does the gathering for you. When you ask a question, it comes back in plain language: what happened, why it matters and what to do next, with the source behind every figure so you can check it.

You keep the decision. Arthur asks before it sends a message or changes anything. The time you used to spend assembling the picture goes back to running the place.

Less time assembling the picture.
More time living in it.

We started LOVELEEDAY to give people back hours in their day: time to look up from the screen, notice the world around them, and spend it with the people and places they love.

How we hold ourselves
  1. Evidence before confidence

    Every figure points back to the record it came from.

  2. History stays intact

    Nothing the organization knew is overwritten or lost.

  3. Uncertainty stays visible

    Where sources disagree or a record is missing, the answer says so.

  4. People keep the decision

    Arthur asks for your approval before it sends a message or changes a record.
