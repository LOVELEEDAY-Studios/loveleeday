A magazine read before it’s an order form — Julienne’s recipe-article layout and Squarespace’s bold editorial hero, in service of a menu that has a story worth reading before you tap Order.

Live at Dabney — the room this menu is written for.

Southern Contemporary Kitchen, at 344 N Rose St, is one of three things Dabney & Co. does — alongside craft cocktails and live music — and the one most often eaten in a hurry, out of a delivery bag, with no story attached. This direction argues the story should travel with the order, not get left at the door.

Start with the Dabney Steakhouse Sandwich $13 — thin-sliced roast beef, Swiss, tomato jam and horseradish cream on a toasted brioche bun. It is the kitchen’s answer to a question most Southern menus don’t ask: what does a steakhouse look like handed across a bar instead of a white tablecloth?

Then the Spicy Fried Chicken & Cornbread $10, a plate built to be loud — crisp fried chicken over jalapeño cornbread, maple drizzle cutting the heat. It is the single most-ordered item across three delivery apps, for a plain reason: it survives a bag better than almost anything else on the menu.

Named for John Dabney — an enslaved Virginia caterer and bartender whose craft was renowned while his freedom was withheld. Southern Hospitality, Liberated. dabneyandco.com/about, quoted verbatim

The name is not a garnish. The site’s own about page is direct about it: “We take our name from John Dabney — and carry it as an ethic, not an ornament.” A menu page that tells that story once, plainly, before asking for a card number, is doing something the DoorDash listing never will.

For the table, not the takeout box: the Sweet Heat Fried Chicken Flatbread $11, mozzarella and a spicy maple drizzle over crisp flatbread — built for the room in the photograph above, where the kitchen and the bandstand share a Tuesday-through-Saturday calendar.
