LOVELEEDAY · Photography brief

Two days of real working life, caught as it happens.

We are looking for one documentary or editorial photographer to shoot a two-day commission at three friendly workplaces in the United States. This page is everything we know today. Please read it, then tell us what is unclear, what you would change, and what it would cost.

1. Purpose

LOVELEEDAY helps owners and operators of small and mid-size organizations see their own data clearly. The people we serve run shops, plants, schools, property offices, counters and kitchens. Our website has to show those people at work, truthfully, so that a visitor thinks one thing: that is my working day.

Every photograph we publish has one job and one test: would an owner or operator in the United States recognize their own working day in it. We choose real work over staged poses, American places over foreign ones, and one grade over many. The photographs will lead our home page and industry pages, and appear in proposals, social posts, decks and ads.

Our current library cannot be fixed by cropping. Of about 60 distinct images, none meets our 2400 px hero minimum, and several industries have no photograph of a person at all (schools and charters, local government, property, portfolios). This commission fills the key gaps. Stock will cover the long tail.

2. The look

Our photography chapter sets the standard. The short version: real people, real rooms, good light.

What we want

  • Unposed work. A named role doing a real task in their own place, looking at the work and not the lens, hands busy, with an object the viewer can read: a clipboard, a counter, a stock bin, a run sheet.
  • Daylight with soft falloff. Warm, slightly muted. Not neon, not smoky, not flat grey, and not cold fluorescent. Use windows and open doors, and switch off mixed-color fixtures where the host allows it.
  • One clear focal point, with faces given room. No cut heads, eyes or hands. Every frame must survive a crop to 3:2, 16:9 and 4:5.
  • Quiet space for type. For hero frames, put the subject on the right third and leave about 40% calm space on the left.
  • Plainly American. Signage, outlets, vehicles, paperwork and people should read as the United States. Working people of varied age, race, gender and build, never tokenism and never a model look.

The grade we will apply

We apply one grade to every original, so please do not grade for us. For reference, it targets white balance near 5300 K, a gentle S-curve with blacks lifted to about 6% and highlights rolled to about 96%, shadows tinted slightly toward navy and highlights toward cream and copper, global saturation down 8%, and neon vests and blue crates down 30%. It adds no grain and no sharpening. Frames that start neutral and honest take it best.

3. The shot list

Thirty numbered shots. Priority A shots are the ones the site cannot launch without. Priority B shots are strongly wanted and are dropped first if time runs short. Where a shot says 3:2 + 4:5, shoot it loose enough that both ratios can be cut from one frame: compose for the 4:5 centre and keep the 3:2 edges useful. Please shoot a few variations of each setup (tight, medium, a little wider), at least two moments of action, and one clean frame where no one looks at the camera.

Shoot with a 35 or 50 mm lens at f/2.8 to f/4 (85 mm for the portraits). No flash. No tilt-shift. Hands relaxed, not pointing.

4. Must avoid

  • The cliches: handshakes, pointing at a screen, a laughing group around a laptop, glowing globes or brains, skylines at sunrise, printed bar charts, coins.
  • Posed, looking-at-camera work, except for the six portraits.
  • Any visible third-party brand, logo or competitor name. Tape over it or frame around it.
  • Foreign flags, signage, languages or landmarks, and any local geography in frame or in a caption.
  • Identifiable minors without a signed guardian release.
  • Headlines, logos or type of any kind added to the picture.
  • Upscaling, sharpening, AI generation or AI extension of any kind. We need the real original.
  • Cold fluorescent, neon, smoky or orange-cast light left uncorrected in the file. Shoot it as clean as the room allows.
  • Empty rooms where a person at work belongs. A place supports a person and never replaces one.

7. Usage rights requested

Please quote a license to LOVELEEDAY Studios LLC for perpetual, worldwide use of all delivered images on web, social, print and advertising, including paid ads, decks, proposals and sales materials, with the right to crop, grade and edit. We do not need exclusivity unless you quote it. If you would charge extra for exclusivity, or for any limit on term, territory or media, list it as a separate line so we can compare. We will not use any image to suggest that a person endorses LOVELEEDAY. Subjects are described only by the role shown.
