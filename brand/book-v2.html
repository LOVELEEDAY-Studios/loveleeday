00 How this book was made

Built the way a launch team builds one.

Version 1 was a style guide for one website. A company launching a product ships something larger: one system that the brand, the software, the site and every launch asset are all cut from. This is that system, and this page is the plan behind it.

Out: the findings below. Three different action colors, three hearts in one folder.

What the audit found

Read straight from the code that serves each surface today. Every one of these is the kind of drift a shared system exists to stop.

Three action colors
The live site's primary button is --btn-blue:#0066cc in site.css. The client portal uses #0B504F, under a token still named --accent-orange. Version 1 of this book says teal dark #0A6261.
Decision. One action color everywhere: sys.action = teal dark #0A6261. Blue is retired; the portal token is renamed.
Three hearts in one folder
public/brand/ holds a dotted heart, a circuit heart and the furry ink heart. Only the ink heart (mark-ink-512.png) ships in the live header, and it is the one that was nearly replaced.
Decision. The ink heart is the only mark. The other files move to _retired/ so nobody picks them up again.
Two headlines, two moods
The original said "Pure intelligence. For a fuller life." over architecture. The live site says "Know what happened. Know what to do next." over a flat ground.
Decision. The original line leads as the brand. The plain-language line becomes the product's promise, one scroll down, beside the answer card.
The software has no brand
The portal's tokens were set separately from the site's, so the product a customer signs into does not look like the company they signed with.
Decision. Part Two of this book. The product reads the same tokens as the site.

01 Platform

Who it is for, and how it should feel.

The platform is the page a designer reads when two good options disagree. If an option breaks one of these, it loses.

1

The answer is the interface

Lead with the sentence that says what happened. Charts and tables support it; they never replace it.

2

Show where it came from

Every answer names its sources. Trust is earned in the small print, so the small print is designed.

3

Calm by default

Cream ground, one action color, one moving thing. Urgency is reserved for things that are actually urgent.

4

Real over rendered

Real people, real rooms, real numbers. Nothing generated to look like a business.

LOVELEEDAY is

  • WarmA well-run studio, not a server room
  • CertainSays the thing, then stops
  • EditorialSerif headlines, room to breathe
  • PlainWords an owner would use out loud

LOVELEEDAY is not

  • TechyNo glowing grids, no neon, no code rain
  • HypedNo "unlock", "empower", "seamless"
  • BusyNo KPI wall on the first screen
  • TemplatedNo three icon cards in a row
02 Brand essence

A calm place to understand your own business.

LOVELEEDAY builds Arthur, an intelligence that reads what an organization already holds and answers in plain language. The brand has to feel like that: unhurried, warm, certain.

Pure intelligence. For a fuller life.

Pure intelligence

The product is the answer, so the surface gets out of the way. Space, one clear sentence at a time, and a single signature image. Nothing that decorates, nothing that performs cleverness.

For a fuller life

The reason it exists. Less time reconciling and chasing, more time with the people and the work that matter. Photography shows that life, never a dashboard pretending to be it.

Warm, not cold

Cream ground, navy ink, one teal. A technology company that feels like a well-run studio.

The voice in one line

Say what happened, say what it means, say what to do. Then stop.

Pairing rules

One statement face per screen. Sentient never sets body copy, labels or buttons. Switzer never sets the hero statement.

Headings tighten as they grow: tracking goes more negative with size, and line-height shrinks toward 1.0. Body stays at zero tracking.

Figures use tabular numerals so columns align.

06 Spacing and grid

An 8-point rhythm, and room to breathe.

Every gap is a multiple of eight. Sections are separated by ninety-six. Content lives in a 1080 px column inside a 40 px gutter, and the page is allowed to be uneven where the content is.

Column

1080 px content, centered. 1280 px for cover and full-bleed rows. Gutter 40 px, 20 px on a phone.

Twelve columns, 16 px gap

Split 7 and 5, or 8 and 4. Never 6 and 6 for the primary idea: give the main thing more space.

Radii

8 px chips and inputs, 12 px cards, 16 px panels, full pill for buttons. One shadow only: 0 24px 56px at 7 percent navy, for floating panels.

Grounding: --s1…--s6, --section-space: 96px, --content: 1080px, --gutter: 40px, --column-gap: 64px, --header-height: 72px and the 8/12/16/32 radii, all read from site.css.

07 Photography

Real people, real rooms, good light.

Photography carries the "fuller life" half of the promise. It is warm, honest and full-bleed, and it always matches the sentence beside it.

The industry set

Nine sectors, each shown by the people who work in it. These replace the text list of industries.

Always

  • Warm light. Golden hour, window light, tungsten. Nothing blue-cold or flash-flat.
  • People in context. Working, deciding, gathering. Faces turned to the task, not to the camera.
  • Full-bleed. Edge to edge, 16:9 on heroes, 3:2 and 1:1 inside the page. Corners at 12 px when inset.
  • The photo answers the sentence. If the line is about a school, the picture is a school.

Never

  • Stock cliche. Handshakes, headsets, glowing globes, a laptop on a white desk.
  • Upscaled or sharpened. Place files at native pixels. Synthetic grain reads as fake.
  • Generated imagery. No AI-made photos and no AI-made new angles of real rooms.
  • Filters over the palette. Do not tint photographs to match teal.

Grounding: all images are real files in public/site/assets/ (hero, architecture, outdoors, dinner, hero-decision and the nine industry-* files). The "photo matches the words" rule is from the 2026-10-10 Doorlight lesson.

08 The brain

One signature image, used once.

The particle brain is how LOVELEEDAY shows a thing that cannot be photographed: records across a business connecting into one answer. It earns its place by appearing rarely.

Specification

  • Lives on the navy stage only. The asset is opaque, so it never sits on cream.
  • Live version is the canvas in brain.js, slow and ambient. It is the one ambient motion on the site.
  • Points in teal, cream and copper from the palette. Multicolor rainbow rendering is retired.
  • Never behind a headline. It gets its own band, with the question and the answer beside it.

12 Charts

One color says "look here".

Charts use the brand palette and nothing else. Everything is neutral until it matters, then one bar or one line turns teal or copper.

Highlight

The series that answers the question is teal. Everything else is line gray.

Attention

Copper marks the thing that needs a person. Never red unless money is already lost.

Sequential

For heat maps and density. Cream to navy through teal, five steps.

Categories

At most four. Past four, the chart is the wrong chart.

Label the data, drop the legend

Names sit at the end of the line or on top of the bar. A legend is a last resort.

Start bars at zero

Lines may crop; bars never do. Gridlines are line gray, three at most.

The sentence goes on top

Every chart has a one-line headline that says what it shows. "October was the best month since May."

17 Motion and voice

Move when asked. Speak like a person.

Both rules come from the same place: respect for the reader. Nothing animates to be noticed, and nothing is said to sound clever.

One entrance

A single staggered reveal on load, 240 to 400 ms. No fade-and-slide on every section.

Response only

After load, things move because someone acted: hover, press, open, answer.

Named properties

Transition color, opacity and transform. Never all. No bounce, no count-up numbers, no drifting blobs.

The brain is the exception

It drifts slowly because it is the product thinking. Everything honors reduced-motion.

cubic-bezier(.2, .7, .2, 1)

The one easing curve. Fast out of the gate, a long soft landing. 200 ms for color, 400 ms for movement.

18 Tells we refuse

If it looks like a template, it is not finished.

A tell is not a bad pattern. It is an undecided one: the statistical middle of the web, applied without a reason. Each of these was found on this site in the 2026-10-11 crawl of 35 pages, and each has a written replacement.

01

The two-tone headline

Second line in gray. 46 percent of our headings did it.

InsteadOne color, one weight. Hierarchy comes from size and space.
02

The two-part slogan

"Short sentence. Short sentence." 29 percent of headings.

InsteadOne honest sentence a founder would say aloud.
03

Four identical cards

Same radius, height and copy length, in a row of three or four.

InsteadA primary and a supporting card at 7 to 5, or a photo that leads.
04

Fake browser chrome

Three traffic-light dots over a mock screen.

InsteadThe real component, on the real ground. The answer card needs no frame.
05

The default font stack

100 percent of characters in the system stack.

InsteadSwitzer and Sentient, with a written reason for each.
06

No type scale

Thirteen different h2 sizes, one weight, one tracking value.

InsteadThe nine-step scale in section 04, tracking tightening with size.
07

Tracked-caps labels everywhere

562 across the site, 85 percent at 10 px.

Instead13 px sentence case, only where a section needs a name.
08

Pills that carry no state

351 chips and status dots, many saying "Live" about nothing.

InsteadA chip appears only when it reports something true.
09

Intentional color, not accidental

Purple gradients, glow, and a new accent every page.

InsteadCream, navy, teal at 60, 30, 10. No gradients on type. No glow.
10

The stock neural-network brain

Glowing rainbow nodes on black, everywhere.

InsteadOne palette-true brain, on one navy band, once.
11

Motion on everything

Fade-up on every block, bounce, count-up stats.

InsteadOne entrance. Motion only in response to the reader.
12

Filler verbs and invented proof

Unlock, empower, seamless. "47 percent faster." Acme Corp.

InsteadName what it does. Delete any number we cannot source.
13

The template page order

Centered hero, badge, two buttons, logo row, three cards, pricing.

InsteadPhotograph, promise, proof, industries as people, a conversation.

Source: knowledge/design/ai-made-website-tells-mastery.md (researched 2026-10-11, measured by ai-tell-check on 35 public pages). Its warning applies to this book too: removing tells does not remove slop. A direction has to be written down, and this book is that direction. The palette was never a tell, and it stays.

Part Four

Keeping it true.

A system drifts the day after launch unless someone owns it. These are the rules for changing the book and the product.

19 Governance

The book changes first.

If a page needs something the book does not have, the book gets it first, with a reason. Then the page. That order is what keeps the site and the software looking like one company.

1

Tokens only

No raw hex values in page code. A check fails the build on any color outside tokens.css.

2

Render before review

Desktop at 1440 and phone at 390, screenshotted and critiqued before anyone is asked to look.

3

Team review

Brand, product and content each sign off. A change that only one discipline has seen is not ready.

4

Measured access

Contrast computed, focus visible, targets 44 px, reduced motion honored. Measured, never eyeballed.
