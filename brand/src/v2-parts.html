00 How this book was made

Built the way a launch team builds one.

Version 1 was a style guide for one website. A company launching a product ships something larger: one system that the brand, the software, the site and every launch asset are all cut from. This is that system, and this page is the plan behind it.

1 · Discover

Audit what exists

Every surface side by side: the original site, the live site, the client portal, the logo folder, social posts.

Out: the findings below. Three different action colors, three hearts in one folder.
2 · Define

Write the platform

Who it is for, the promise, the personality, four principles. Every design call is checked against these.

Out: chapters 01 and 02. One page that settles arguments.
3 · Express

Lock the identity

Logo, color, type, imagery, the brain. Kept from version 1 where Daniel already approved it, unchanged.

Out: chapters 03 to 08.
4 · Systematize

Turn it into tokens

Named values in three tiers so the site and the software read from the same file and cannot drift apart again.

Out: chapter 09. One token file for both codebases.
5 · Apply

Product, site, launch

The app shell, components, charts and states. The page anatomy for the site. Social, deck and email cut from the same parts.

Out: chapters 10 to 18.
6 · Govern

Keep it true

Owners, review gates, versioning. The book changes first, then the product. Never the reverse.

Out: chapter 19.

The team this book stands in for

Google, Apple and the studios that build launches for them staff these seats. Each chapter is written from one of them, and each was reviewed against the others before it went in.

Brand design

Logo, color, type, imagery. Owns chapters 03 to 08.

Product design

The software: shell, components, states, density.

Design systems engineering

Tokens and the shared CSS both codebases import.

Content design

Voice, labels, empty and error copy, plain language.

Motion

Timing, easing, and the one thing allowed to drift: the brain.

Data visualization

Chart colors and rules for numbers people act on.

Accessibility

Measured contrast, focus, target size, reduced motion.

Research

Five owners from different industries react before anything is final.

What the audit found

Read straight from the code that serves each surface today. Every one of these is the kind of drift a shared system exists to stop.

Three action colors
The live site's primary button is --btn-blue:#0066cc in site.css. The client portal uses #0B504F, under a token still named --accent-orange. Version 1 of this book says teal dark #0A6261.
Decision. One action color everywhere: sys.action = teal dark #0A6261. Blue is retired; the portal token is renamed.
Three hearts in one folder
public/brand/ holds a dotted heart, a circuit heart and the furry ink heart. Only the ink heart (mark-ink-512.png) ships in the live header, and it is the one that was nearly replaced.
Decision. The ink heart is the only mark. The other files move to _retired/ so nobody picks them up again.
Two headlines, two moods
The original said "Pure intelligence. For a fuller life." over architecture. The live site says "Know what happened. Know what to do next." over a flat ground.
Decision. The original line leads as the brand. The plain-language line becomes the product's promise, one scroll down, beside the answer card.
The software has no brand
The portal's tokens were set separately from the site's, so the product a customer signs into does not look like the company they signed with.
Decision. Part Two of this book. The product reads the same tokens as the site.

Method sources. Google says its 2025 Material 3 Expressive work ran 46 studies with more than 18,000 participants before the system shipped, using eye tracking, surveys and usability tests (Chrome Unboxed, Android Authority, both reporting Google's own post). Google gave Gemini one signature expression, a gradient that shows the assistant thinking, and then carried it into the company "G" (Google, 9to5Google); the brain plays that role here. The three-tier token model (reference, system, component) follows Material's naming as described by Uxcel and TomTom. Audit values read from public/site/assets/site.css and arthur-launch/app/globals.css on 2026-10-18.

11 Charts

One color says "look here".

Charts use the brand palette and nothing else. Everything is neutral until it matters, then one bar or one line turns teal or copper.

Highlight

The series that answers the question is teal. Everything else is line gray.

Attention

Copper marks the thing that needs a person. Never red unless money is already lost.

Sequential

For heat maps and density. Cream to navy through teal, five steps.

Categories

At most four. Past four, the chart is the wrong chart.

Label the data, drop the legend

Names sit at the end of the line or on top of the bar. A legend is a last resort.

Start bars at zero

Lines may crop; bars never do. Gridlines are line gray, three at most.

The sentence goes on top

Every chart has a one-line headline that says what it shows. "October was the best month since May."

Part Four

Keeping it true.

A system drifts the day after launch unless someone owns it. These are the rules for changing the book and the product.

Gates and versionsToken source
18 Governance

The book changes first.

If a page needs something the book does not have, the book gets it first, with a reason. Then the page. That order is what keeps the site and the software looking like one company.

1

Tokens only

No raw hex values in page code. A check fails the build on any color outside tokens.css.

2

Render before review

Desktop at 1440 and phone at 390, screenshotted and critiqued before anyone is asked to look.

3

Team review

Brand, product and content each sign off. A change that only one discipline has seen is not ready.

4

Measured access

Contrast computed, focus visible, targets 44 px, reduced motion honored. Measured, never eyeballed.
