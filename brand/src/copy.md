## 7. Band 5: How it works

Headline: **From one question to a figure you can defend.** [live: nav "From your first question to a figure you can defend"]

Three steps
1. **Send a short brief.** Tell us the decision you need to make and what records you have. We reply in writing, aiming for one working day. [how-it-works.html]
2. **We answer one question.** We connect one system, read-only, and answer it with the source behind every figure. You get a written map of what else could be read. [how-it-works.html; index.html "Intelligence Audit"]
3. **Your team asks for itself.** Your team asks across its core systems and checks the records behind each answer. We set up access, train the people who will use it, and stay alongside as things change. [how-it-works.html]

Promise line: Your systems stay yours. We connect them read-only, one at a time, keep your data separate and in the United States, and delete it when you ask. [trust.html]

## 10. Brand book chapter 14

Chapter headline: **One home page, built from the two we loved.**
Lead (35 words): The original site gave us the feeling. The live site gave us the proof. This chapter joins them: a photograph and a promise first, then one answer with its sources, then Arthur, then your industry.

Rule notes (one per band, plain language)
- Band 1, hero: The headline is fixed and the photograph carries the feeling. One promise, one main button. Nothing else competes in the first screen, because the first job is to make someone breathe out.
- Band 2, promise and proof: A claim is only believed once it has a worked example. One answer card, its sources named, replaces a row of feature cards that say the same thing without proof.
- Band 3, Arthur: The brain is the one thing allowed to move, because it is the product thinking. It sits next to a plain question so the motion always has a reason.
- Band 4, industries: Nine tiles, nine different worries, no repeats of any question elsewhere. A visitor should find their own world in under five seconds, and never think this is only for one kind of business.
- Band 5, how it works: Three steps, one line each, then the safety promise at the point where doubt appears. Fear of handing over data is answered here, not hidden in a footer.
- Band 6, closing: Same warm tone as the opening, one clear next step. The page ends where it began, with a life, not a pitch.

## Why

- **The headline stays and the subhead carries the product.** The original's feeling lives in "Pure intelligence. For a fuller life." The live subhead already says what the product does in one plain sentence, so it is kept almost whole.
- **Every band answers one question the visitor has.** What is this, can I trust it, what is Arthur, is it for me, what happens next, and what do I do now. No band repeats another band's job.
- **Questions are asked the way an owner says them out loud.** Each industry question names a worry (a job that ran long, a lease that ends, a volunteer who left) instead of describing a method, following VOICE.md.
- **Answers state what happened, what it means, what to do.** The example card ends on "Call those two customers today", the pattern from the book's voice chapter.
- **Industries span the economy.** A school, a road crew, a shelf, a lease and a volunteer sit side by side, so no visitor reads the page as written for one trade. Dabney and bars are not used anywhere.
- **Claims come from the live pages only.** Read-only connection, separate and US-hosted data, deletion on request, written reply in about a day are all quoted from how-it-works and trust. Nothing new is promised.
- **Warm, certain, short.** Plain verbs, short sentences, one idea per line, and buttons that say what they do ("See how it works", "Start a project").
- **The original's best idea comes back as the interaction, not as decoration.** The "Ask simply" tabs and the four-question brain strip let a visitor watch one name become one company, which no screenshot can do.
