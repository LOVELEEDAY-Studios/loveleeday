07 Photography

Real people, real rooms, good light.

Every photograph has one job and one test: would an owner or operator in the United States recognise their own working day in it. We choose real work over staged poses, American places over foreign ones, and one grade over many.

The strategy: four kinds of photograph

Each pillar answers three questions: when we use it, what it shows, and why it earns a place. A photograph that fits none of them is cut. The mix across the library is about 60% people at work, 25% places, 15% objects.

People at work 60% / places 25% / objects 15%

Pillar 1

The Working Day

When

Home hero, industry heroes, Talk and Contact, proposals. One per page.

What

A named role doing a real task in their own place. Unposed, looking at the work and not the lens, hands busy, with an object the viewer can read, such as a clipboard, a counter or a stock bin.

Why

The first question a buyer asks is whether this is for someone like them. Real working people hold attention; generic stock is ignored (Nielsen Norman Group). Amplitude and Cloudflare cast "not models" for the same reason.

Pillar 2

The Place

When

Section bands, concept pages, industry supporting images. Never an industry hero when a person-at-work frame exists.

What

A room a customer would recognise, plausibly American, in daylight, with strong lines and lived-in detail. People are small or absent. Timber, stone, glass, a council desk.

Why

Environments let us cover nine industries without staging faces (Cloudflare). Both reviews also warn that empty buildings cannot carry a sector, so a place supports a person and never replaces one.

Pillar 3

The Quiet Moment

When

The life band and the closing band. One per page, two on the whole site.

What

A small human beat the product gives back: a parent and child outdoors, a quiet table, a journal and a cup. Warm, slightly muted, with the subject large enough to read.

Why

The tagline, "For a fuller life", needs a picture of the promise. This is the only place the brand may be emotional, so it is rationed.

Pillar 4

The Detail

When

Notes, trust, architecture, deck backgrounds and social. Free libraries are acceptable here and nowhere else.

What

A tactile object or space that carries the metaphor of the headline beside it: archive drawers for records, packed boxes for migration, a catalogue corridor for lineage.

Why

The best-curated material on the site today. Texture gives editorial rhythm without stock metaphors such as globes, brains and handshakes. It must still match the words next to it.

Proof is not a photograph. A claim is shown with a portal screenshot or the answer card, never stock. The particle brain is brand art, not a photo, and does not sit beside photographs.

Hard rejects, whatever the score

  • Made or extended by a generator, including Midjourney and "new angles" of real rooms.
  • Unlicensed, watermarked, or a minor without a signed release.
  • A visible third-party brand or logo, such as the sign on the skyline we cut.
  • A foreign landmark, flag, sign or language, or any local geography in the caption.
  • Headline or logo baked into the picture. Type is always live.
  • Upscaled or sharpened to reach a size. Re-source the original instead.

One grade

The set swings from cold fluorescent to neon to smoky, so nothing reads as one shoot. A single grade, applied to originals before export, fixes that. The previews below are a CSS approximation; the real grade is one LUT.

White balance. 5300 K, neutral-warm. Fluorescent or cool frames go 350 to 500 K warmer. Amber frames go 300 K cooler.
Contrast. Gentle S-curve. Blacks lifted to about 6%, never crushed. Highlights rolled to about 96% so whites land near cream, not paper white.
Split tone. Shadows toward navy at 6 to 8%. Highlights toward cream and copper at 4%.
Saturation. Global minus 8%. Reds and oranges minus 18%. Yellow-greens minus 15%. Neon vests and blue crates minus 30%. Teal 0%. Skin protected by a hue mask.
Grain and sharpness. None added. Never sharpen, never upscale. Reduce only.
Delivery. Apply the LUT to originals, then export 2400, 1880 and 1200 px with a 4:5 crop of each, and rebuild the WebP files.

Gaps and the shot list

The library cannot be fixed by cropping. The recommendation is one commissioned two-day documentary shoot for the key slots, with named stock libraries for the long tail.

  • Commission. One US documentary or editorial photographer, two days, three locations from friendly clients: a regional manufacturer or distributor, a charter school or district office, a property or retail office. Signed model and location releases. Confirm with two quotes first.
  • Deliver. 4000 px or wider, unretouched, so the grade is applied in one pass. 35 or 50 mm at f/2.8 to f/4, no flash, no tilt-shift. Hands relaxed and not pointing. No logos, no foreign signage, no legible brands.
  • Stock for the long tail. Stocksy United (candid, exclusive licence available for heroes), Offset, Cavan Images, Tetra Images and Moment, Nappy, Death to Stock, and Adobe Stock Premium only with strict filters.
  • Filters and limits. Search "candid" and "documentary" with US nouns such as "charter school office". Pexels and Unsplash serve objects and textures for Notes only, never a hero or an industry page.
  1. Home hero. An owner and one colleague mid-task in a real shop or school office, reading a legible run sheet or receipt together. Right third, 40% empty on the left, daylight. 3:2 and 4:5 from one frame. No printed chart, no handshake, no lone person looking down.
  2. Manufacturing. A plant manager and line lead at a US shop-floor workstation with a printed job traveler. Tidy floor, daylight, safety yellow only as an accent.
  3. Schools and charters. Office staff with an attendance binder; a parent at the front counter; a teacher in a corridor with kids' artwork. No identifiable students without releases, no flags in focus.
  4. Local government. A clerk and a resident at a US service counter; a public works crew at a truck bed with a map; a council chamber with people in it.
  5. Property. A manager walking a vacant unit with a tablet held low, and a leasing desk with keys and a folder. Soft open shade, no cars at the edge, no air conditioners.
  6. Portfolios and credit. A principal with three printed statements and a coffee at a window table, or three storefront doors. No skyline, no stock charts, no coins.
  7. Operations and pricing. An owner closing the day with a receipt printout and a calculator; hand-counted stock in daylight. Readable paper, US signage.
  8. Retail and hospitality, not bars. An e-commerce packing table; a hotel front desk or housekeeping lead; a banquet manager setting a room in daylight.
  9. Nonprofit and professional services. A pantry intake desk with a coordinator; a partner and client at a table over documents. A different shoot from the existing nonprofit frames.
  10. Life. A long table meal in daylight with mixed ages and no restaurant branding; a parent and child outdoors, large in frame.
  11. Six portraits. Owners and operators at their own workplaces across retail, manufacturing, school, property, nonprofit and hospitality. 85 mm, f/2.8, 4:5, window light camera-left, eyes in the top third.
  12. Objects and the team. Archive boxes labeled by year; hands passing a folder; the real LOVELEEDAY team at a table, named only with permission.
  13. Share cards, not a photo gap. Rebuild the two broken cards from clean frames and live type. Test the title box at 1200 by 630 with 80 px margins.
